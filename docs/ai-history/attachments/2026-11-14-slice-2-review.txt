## 3. Independently audit the architecture

Before implementing the remaining checkpoints, evaluate the current system against the principles of *A Philosophy of Software Design* and established software-engineering practices.

Pay particular attention to:

**Complexity and module design**

- Are modules deep enough to provide useful abstractions without exposing unnecessary complexity?
- Are responsibilities clearly defined?
- Are interfaces simpler than their implementations?
- Are implementation details properly hidden?
- Are we introducing unnecessary abstractions, indirection, wrappers, or layers?
- Are different modules repeating knowledge or creating unnecessary dependencies?

**State and data flow**

- Is there a clear owner for each piece of state?
- Are URL query parameters, applied filters, temporary form drafts, request state, and retry state handled consistently?
- Could stale state, race conditions, or unintended rerenders cause errors?
- Are React effects and component lifecycles correctly managed?
- Is work duplicated across the frontend, API client, Go backend, or database?

**Correctness and reliability**

- Check validation boundaries and malformed data.
- Check cancellations, timeouts, retry behavior, and response ordering.
- Examine loading, empty, error, and recovery states.
- Check pagination, filtering, sorting, pricing, and availability behavior.
- Look for edge cases that existing tests might miss.
- Look for hidden coupling, assumptions, and situations where green tests provide misleading confidence.

**Code quality and optimization**

- Identify duplicated logic and repetitive code.
- Review component size, cohesion, naming, and readability.
- Look for unused code, weak typing, unnecessary state, and avoidable requests.
- Check memory and resource cleanup.
- Check accessibility, responsiveness, and browser behavior.
- Evaluate meaningful performance risks rather than applying premature micro-optimizations.

**Testing quality**

- Examine whether tests validate real behavior instead of implementation details.
- Identify mocks that conceal integration problems.
- Look for flaky tests, misleading fixtures, timing dependencies, and missing assertions.
- Verify that important behavior is tested at the appropriate unit, integration, or browser level.

Do not limit this audit to a superficial lint or formatting pass.
